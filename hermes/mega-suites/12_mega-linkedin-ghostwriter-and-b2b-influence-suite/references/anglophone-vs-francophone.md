# Anglophone vs Francophone Copywriting Traditions & Cultural Nuances

Two distinct lineages converge in modern business writing and B2B ghostwriting, but conventions remain fundamentally different. Literal translation of American hooks and openings into French almost always fails. Use this reference when writing in French, adapting US frameworks, or creating bilingual publications.

---

## 1. The Two Distinct Lineages

### The Anglophone Direct-Response Lineage
- **Key Authors:** Claude Hopkins (*Scientific Advertising*, 1923), John Caples (*Tested Advertising Methods*, 1932), David Ogilvy, Eugene Schwartz (*Breakthrough Advertising*, 1966), Gary Halbert (*The Boron Letters*, 1984), Joseph Sugarman (*The Adweek Copywriting Handbook*), Bob Bly.
- **Modern Proponents:** Packy McCormick, Lenny Rachitsky, Morgan Housel, Shaan Puri, Justin Welsh.
- **Center of Gravity:** Direct response, psychological persuasion levers, emotional agitation, high velocity, and transactional momentum.

### The Francophone Lineage (Two Separated Schools)
1. **The Journalistic School:**
   - CFJ, ESJ Lille, Yves Agnès (*Manuel de journalisme*), Benoît Grevisse (*Écriture journalistique*).
   - Core concepts: *L'attaque*, *le chapô*, *l'accroche*, *la chute*, *la pyramide inversée*, les *5W* (Qui, Quoi, Où, Quand, Pourquoi).
   - Principle: *"L'attaque doit être palpable, habitée, elle doit attirer et captiver le lecteur sans jamais lui mentir."*
2. **The Modern French Copywriting School:**
   - Sélim Niederhoffer (*Le Guide du copywriting*, Eyrolles), Stéphane Truphème (*Captain Marketing*), Christopher Piton, Maud Alaves.
   - Core approach: Adapts US direct response to French cultural psychology by tempering hyperbolic promises, removing cheap theatrics, and emphasizing craft, legitimate authority, and intellectual rigor.

## 2. What Works in English but Fails in French

| Anglo Technique | Why It Breaks in French | Cultural Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Heavy Second-Person ("YOU need this")** | Reads aggressive, condescending, and excessively American. Triggers instant skepticism. | Use "vous" sparingly. Prefer "on", "nous", or neutral third-person observation. |
| **Hyperbolic Promises ("This one trick will...")** | Triggers *réactance* (psychological resistance) immediately. French B2B buyers have higher baseline cynicism. | Replace with concrete data, modest understatement (*litote*), or real field metrics. |
| **Single-Sentence Paragraphs throughout** | Reads as infantile, breathless, and uneducated (*"écriture hachée à l'américaine"*). | Mix punchy 1-line sentences with structured 2-to-3 sentence paragraphs. |
| **All-Caps, Dollar Signs & Urgency Alarms** | Associated immediately with spam, crypto scams, and dropshipping gurus. | Absolute prohibition of ALL-CAPS in bodies. Use bold or italics very sparingly. |
| **Stoic / Philosopher Quotes as Life Lessons** | In English, a Ryan Holiday Marcus Aurelius quote feels inspiring; in French, it feels pretentious and pedantic. | Replace quotes with firsthand operational anecdotes and specific mistakes. |

## 3. What Works in French but Fails in English

| Franco Technique | Why It Breaks in English | Anglo Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Slow Descriptive Scene Setting (*Plan Large*)** | English readers bounce in 3 seconds. Online English demands immediate payoff. | Lead with the finding or metric first; place the scene after the thesis. |
| **Complex Subordinate Clauses (*Phrases Périodiques*)** | Perceived in English as ChatGPT slop, bureaucratic fog, or cognitive load. | Break into SVO (Subject-Verb-Object) atomic sentences. |
| **Literary Inversions & Elevated Prose** | Difficult to render; English syntax resists stylistic grammatical inversions. | Keep natural forward subject order. |
| **Abstract Conceptual Synthesis** | English readers demand actionable takeaways and specific tooling names. | Provide explicit checklists, scripts, and concrete deliverables. |

## 4. Slippery Slide vs Attaque Journalistique

- **Sugarman's Slippery Slide (US):**
  - *Transactional:* Every sentence exists solely to force the reader to read the next sentence. Optimized for micro-commitments and continuous acceleration.
- **L'Attaque Journalistique (FR):**
  - *Contractual:* The opening makes a solemn contract with the reader (promising an angle, a secret revealed, or a demonstrated reality), and the *chute* (ending) closes the contract with symmetry.

### The Golden Ghostwriting Rules:
- **Writing in English for a French author:** The usual mistake is opening too slowly or poetically. *Fix:* Start with the controversial finding or the hard metric, then provide the context.
- **Writing in French for an English author:** The usual mistake is being too transactional and aggressive ("Vous n'allez pas en revenir..."). *Fix:* Replace the aggressive promise with a concrete operational scene, an unexpected contrast, or an undeniable metric.

## 5. Register Quick-Reference Matrix

| Context | Target Register | What to Favor | What to Strictly Avoid |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **French B2B SaaS Post** | Professionnel incisif | Métriques terrain, ironie sobre, vocabulaire technique précis | Promesses miracles, anglicismes creux ("game-changer", "mindset") |
| **French Executive / Founder Post** | Soutenu accessible | Attaque journalistique, chute soignée, paradoxe défendable | Tutoiement, questions de fin paresseuses, listes de puces emojis |
| **English Tech / Dev Post** | Direct, engineer-to-engineer | Code diffs, architecture tradeoffs, benchmarks, SVO sentences | Sentimental drama, broad generalizations, marketing jargon |
| **Cross-Border Newsletter** | Hybride narratif | Scène in medias res, tension chiffrée, analyse post-mortem | Définitions de dictionnaire, rhétorique scolaire (thèse/antithèse) |
